Fig. 1. Effect of the energy term in the canonical scenario (12 vehicles, 100 requests, 825 kg / 80 kg, 100% SOC, 1,800 s window, UTP network, Crr 0.012), replicated on independent workload seeds in every study component. (a) Battery saving; (b) reduction in mean passenger ride time (not recorded in the original 2×2, A or B). Points: paired seed-level means with 95% t intervals. Diamonds: random-effects pooled estimates (post hoc; not prespecified): battery 1.62% [0.91, 2.32] Greedy and 1.70% [1.16, 2.23] Hungarian (340 seeds); ride 13.6 s [11.2, 16.1] and 11.0 s [8.5, 13.5] (160 seeds). *Original 2×2 uses the condition-mean time-only withdrawal as denominator.

Fig. 2. Experiment A: energy saving against the passenger-to-vehicle load ratio ρ for (a) Greedy and (b) Hungarian (100 paired seeds per point). Filled markers, solid lines: whole-run battery saving (time-only minus energy-weighted withdrawal, % of time-only). Open markers, dashed lines: same-state decision-level saving (planned energy of the time-only minus the energy-weighted choice in identical fleet states, equal-cardinality rounds, % of run withdrawal). 95% t intervals. The shaded 160 kg-per-passenger point is a non-physical stress test (exploratory). Prespecified A1 (14.0% vs 7.4%, decision level): +0.67 pp Hungarian, +0.60 pp Greedy, both Holm p = 4×10⁻⁶, below the 1 pp margin.

Fig. 3. Experiment D: response to the energy weight αE relative to the time-only objective (αE = 0), 40 paired seeds per point. (a, b) Battery saving; filled markers are significant after Holm adjustment over the 24-test energy-sweep family, open markers are not. (c, d) Reduction in mean passenger ride time (secondary, unadjusted). 'PK proxy' is the physics-derived passenger-km surrogate (not fitted to outcomes). 95% t intervals. No optimal weight is selected from these confirmatory seeds; mean completions changed by at most 0.05 per 100 requests in any arm.

Fig. 4. Operating boundaries of the whole-run battery saving (40 paired seeds per point, 95% t intervals). Filled markers are significant after Holm adjustment within the experiment's prespecified family; open markers are not significant or, for B at 25% SOC, descriptive (no prespecified energy contrast). B: fleet size and demand scale; E: second network (UM district, synthetic mapped demand) and compressed arrival window; F: rolling-resistance sensitivity informed by external measurements. The prespecified high-road-load SOC interaction for Greedy was −3.18 pp [−4.70, −1.66], Holm p = 0.0022.

Fig. 5. Experiment B: change in completed requests per 100 when initial SOC is reduced from 100% to 25%, by fleet size and demand (40 paired seeds, 95% t intervals). Open markers: time-only objective; filled: energy-weighted. The loss depends on fleet capacity, not on the energy term: prespecified energy-weight × SOC interactions at 24 vehicles lie within ±1 completion per 100.

Fig. 6. Experiment C: decomposition of the whole-run effect into single-round pulses and sequential prefixes (40 seeds, 95% t intervals). The three primary estimands (whole-run, whole-run minus pulse sum, prefix continuation) were all inconclusive after Holm adjustment (open markers); pulse sum and prefix direct component are descriptive. The prefix components sum to the whole-run saving by construction.
